The temperature fallback is a retry rule: it decodes a phrase again when the first result looks bad.

**How it works in faster-whisper**

Each phrase is first decoded at temperature 0, which always picks the most likely token. The result counts as failed if either of these holds:

- **Repetitive text:** the output compresses too well (compression ratio above 2.4). A typical case is a loop like "և և և և…".
- **Low confidence:** the average log-probability per token is below −1.0.

On a failure, faster-whisper decodes the same audio again at temperatures 0.2, 0.4, … up to 1.0. Above 0 it samples five candidates per attempt and keeps the best. It stops at the first attempt that passes. If none pass, it keeps the most confident attempt.

**What we gain by keeping it**
- It can rescue phrases where greedy decoding got stuck in a loop or went off track. Sampling often finds a better path.
- This matters most for exactly the hard cases: noise, crosstalk, unclear speech.

**What it costs**
- **Time, unbounded in practice.** One failure can mean up to 6 full decodes, and the sampled ones decode 5 candidates each. That turns about 1 s into 8 s, which is what broke the soak.
- **The retry isn't necessarily better.** The slow benchmark clip still came out with a broken character (`�`) after the retries. At high temperature, sampled output can be more wrong or invented, not less.
- **Unpredictable latency.** It is rare, but you can't know in advance which phrase will hit it.

**What turning it off means in our setup**
- It fires rarely: by timing, 1 of 200 benchmark clips, and about 1 of 187 soak phrases. So the accuracy effect should be small, but I haven't measured it.
- Two existing settings already reduce what it guards against. `condition_on_previous_text=False` prevents the classic runaway loops, and the transcriber already drops empty and wrong-script output.
- The real risk is a rare greedy loop or a confused phrase being printed as it is, instead of being retried.
- Note that a loop is slow even without retries, because the first decode runs on until its token limit. The fallback doesn't speed that case up.

**Middle grounds, if you want to keep some rescue**
- **One retry only** (temperatures 0 and 0.4). The worst case becomes about two decodes (~2–3 s), which stays inside 5 s.
- **Repetition controls instead of retries.** BuzzASR's model card decodes with `no_repeat_ngram_size=3` and `repetition_penalty=1.2`. These prevent loops during the first decode itself, at almost no time cost.

**What I'd measure before choosing:** run the ASR benchmark with the fallback off, and with one retry. The question is whether WER moves on 200 clips. If both stay near 11.4 %, turning it off is the safer choice for the hard 5 s limit.
