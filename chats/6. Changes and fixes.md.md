Speaker identification merges people for a few reasons, and they add up. I'd fix it in two steps: cheap changes to how the tracker decides, then a measured threshold or a stronger model.

**How it works now (`speakers.py`).** Each call-side phrase becomes a voiceprint, from WeSpeaker ResNet34. That voiceprint is compared with each known speaker's average voiceprint. If the cosine similarity is at least 0.4, the phrase joins that speaker. Every phrase that joins, however short, is then added to that speaker's average.

**Why different people end up as one speaker**
1. **0.4 is a guess and probably too low.** It was never tuned (TODO 2). Everyone on a call comes through the same codec and line, and that shared sound makes different people's voiceprints look more alike than in clean audio.
2. **The averages drift.** Once one wrong phrase joins a speaker, their average moves toward the other person, which pulls in more wrong phrases. A few mistakes early on can merge two people for the rest of the call.
3. **Short phrases give unreliable voiceprints.** A 0.5–1 s reply like "yes" or "okay" can match almost anyone, and today it still updates the average.
4. **The new padding goes into the voiceprint.** Since this session's segmenter change, each phrase carries up to 0.5 s of silence or background, and the voiceprint includes it. That's a small but real dilution.
5. **One phrase can hold two voices.** With quick turn-taking and no pause, two people's speech ends up in one phrase, and the mixed voiceprint matches whoever is closest.
